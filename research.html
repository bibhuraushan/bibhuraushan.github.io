Research

How the Sun's magnetism evolves, cycle after cycle

I connect a century of solar observations with flux transport models to understand why every solar cycle is different, and what that means for space weather.

>>>>>>> Stashed changes

The big question

The Sun's magnetic field is generated deep inside it, surfaces as sunspots, is torn apart and swept around by surface flows, and every eleven years or so flips its polarity. Each cycle is different: some are strong, some weak, and we still cannot reliably say how big the next one will be.

My research follows the field from the surface back in time and forward into forecasts. I calibrate long historical archives into consistent records, use them to drive flux transport models that rebuild the Sun's magnetic field as far back as 1755, and turn the results into open data and tools. The goal is a physical, testable picture of solar variability, and better predictions of the space weather that affects satellites, power grids and astronauts.

Project 1

Observation: Solar Cycle Variability

Question
How and why does each solar cycle differ, and what sets the strength of the next one?
Method
Calibrate century-long archives, especially Kodaikanal white-light, Ca II K and hand-drawn suncharts, into consistent series: sunspot areas, plage butterfly diagrams, rotation rates and polar network proxies for the polar field.
Code and data
KoSO datasets · Unified sunspot groups

Related papers

    Project 2

    Modeling: Solar Cycle Variability

    Question
    What does the Sun's whole surface field look like where we cannot see it: on the far side, at the poles, and before magnetographs existed?
    Method
    The Advective Flux Transport (AFT) model and the open-source HipFT move magnetic flux with observed surface flows and assimilate magnetograms. Synthetic active regions (SARG) fill in the past, from reconstructions back to 1755 to forecasts of the polar field reversal.
    Code and data
    aftpy · HipFT

      Project 3

      Observation: Feature Identification and Tracking

      Question
      How do we turn decades of uneven images and drawings into consistent, documented data that models and machine learning can trust?
      Method
      Automated detection and tracking (AutoTAB for bipolar magnetic regions), neural networks and clustering on hand-drawn suncharts, and cross-calibration with error characterization, released as open datasets and software.
      Code and data
      autotabpy · helioget

      Related papers

        Project 4

        Magnetic Reconnection

        Question
        How does magnetic reconnection release stored magnetic energy so quickly on the Sun?
        Method
        Study transient events and the changes in magnetic connectivity around them, linking the topology of the field to where and how energy is released.

        Project 5

        Spectropolarimetry

        Question
        What can polarized light tell us about the magnetic field and thermodynamics of the solar plasma?
        Method
        Polarized spectral diagnostics and inversions that infer magnetic and thermodynamic properties, with careful handling of their uncertainties.
